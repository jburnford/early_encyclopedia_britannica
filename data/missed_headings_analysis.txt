  letters representing the first letters of the surrounding page range) is a
  systematic OCR artifact from the printed running headers on each page. These
  3-letter stubs are NOT articles; they are the parser picking up the page header
  as if it were an article heading. This accounts for roughly 80–100 of the 1,248
  entries.

====================================================================================================
PATTERNS AND SYSTEMATIC ISSUES
====================================================================================================

1. RUNNING HEADER STUBS (most common false positive type):
   The parser is picking up 2-3 letter running headers from printed book pages
   (e.g., "ALB", "FIN", "GLA", "MUG") as article headings. These appear as
   "XYZ\n\nARTICLE" patterns where XYZ is the running header and ARTICLE is
   the first word of the next article. This accounts for approximately 80-100
   false positives. The fix is to filter headings that are 2-3 letters followed
   immediately by a longer word that IS the actual article heading.

2. DUPLICATE OCR FILES:
   Many missed headings appear 2-3 times because the same volume was OCR'd
   multiple times (BYW, IND, TRE prefix variants). The true count of distinct
   missed article positions is roughly 40-50% of the 1,248 total.

3. PLATE LABELS IN LONG ARTICLES:
   The 1815 and 1823 editions have many "PLATE DIX/DXX/etc." labels embedded
   in technical articles (Steam, Telescope, Galvanism). These are plate captions
   acting as section dividers, not article headings.

4. ANATOMICAL/BOTANICAL SECTION HEADERS:
   Long technical articles (Anatomy, Botany, Chemistry) use internal section
   headers in all-caps (ULNARIS EXTERNUS, INTESTINUM COLON, TRUE RHUBARB)
   that resemble article headings but are not.

5. COPTIC GLOSSARY CLUSTER (1860 v11):
   A concentrated cluster of 15-20 false positives at position 52.1% of v11
   comes from a Coptic vocabulary table (probably within an EGYPT or HIEROGLYPHICS
   article). The 2-4 letter Coptic glosses (MNA, SHA, HTEE, HNEE, etc.) were
   all classified as potential headings. This is a single-source error.

6. CONSISTENT MISSES ACROSS EDITIONS:
   Several genuine article headings are systematically missed across multiple
   editions (BRESCIA, ARCHITREASURER, LATE-WAKE, LANCEROTA, ARICINA). This
   suggests the parser has a structural issue with how certain text patterns
   precede these headings — perhaps they consistently appear after figure
   references, quotations, or footnotes that reset the newline count.

7. 1860 EDITION HAS THE MOST FALSE POSITIVES:
   The 8th edition (1860) has 160 candidates but a higher proportion of genuine
   article starts, partly because the format changed to longer, more distinct
   article separators. The 1771 and 1778 editions have more running-header
   stub false positives due to page layout format.

8. CROSS-REFERENCE ENTRIES:
   A significant minority (perhaps 15-20%) of the candidates are legitimate
   cross-reference entries ("See X") that have headings but no article body.
   These ARE valid headings (LANSKIP, THRUSSLE, CHRYSAL, etc.) and should
   appear in the headword index, but they contain no recoverable article text.

9. INTERNAL EPITAPHS AND INSCRIPTIONS:
   Several entries (JONATHAN SWIFT, WILLIAM WARBURTON, DANIEL PULTENEY, JOHN
   RENNIE) are Latin or English epitaph inscriptions formatted in all-caps within
   biographical articles, not standalone article headings.
