PHILO

Volume 17 · 231 words · 1860 Edition

celebrated writer on mechanics, was a native of Byzantium, and flourished in the year 160, being the contemporary of Ctesibius and Hero. He was the author of a work, entitled Πολεμικόν, on the method of attacking and defending towns, of which the fourth and fifth books have been preserved. The first treats of the manner of preparing arrows, ballista, catapultae, and other warlike engines. Amongst other inventions, he mentions a machine of Ctesibius which discharged weapons by means of compressed air, upon the same principle, no doubt, as our air-gun. In the second book he treats of the manner of fortifying and provisioning cities, and, amongst other things, recommends that the provisions and water be poisoned if there be danger of the place falling into the hands of an enemy. This work has been published, with a Latin translation, in a collection entitled Veteran Mathematicorum Opera, Par, 1683. The invention of the air-gun has been treated of by Alb-Louis Meister, De Catapulta polybolis Commentatio qua locus Philonis Mechanici, in libro iv. de telorum constructione extans illustrator, Göttingen, 1768. There is another little work attributed to Philo, entitled Ἱερὰ ἀπὸ ἑβδομάδος (On the Seven Wonders of the World), part of the sixth chapter of which, and the whole of the seventh, are lost. It has been published, with a Latin translation and many learned notes, by J. C. Orelli, Leipzig, 1816, 8vo.
