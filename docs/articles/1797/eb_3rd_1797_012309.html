KEFISTAH. This word is to be met with in Genesis and in Job, and is translated in the Septuagint and Vulgate "sheep or lambs:" But the Rabbins and modern interpreters are generally of opinion, that kefistah signifies rather a piece of money. Bochart and Eubinus are of opinion the Septuagint meant minas, and not lambs; in Greek hecatonmonon, ἑκατόμνον, instead of ἐκατόν μνᾶς. Now a mina was worth 60 Hebrew shekels, and consequently 61. 16s. 10½ d. Sterling. M. de Pelletier of Rouen is of opinion, that kefistah was a Persian coin, stamped on one side with an archer (Κεφίσταρ or Κεφίσθη in Hebrew signifying "a bow") and on the other with a lamb; that this was a gold coin known in the east by the name of a daric, and was in value about 12 livres and 10 d. French money. Several learned men, without mentioning the value of the kefistah, say it was a silver coin, the impression whereof was a sheep, for which reason the Septuagint and Vulgate translate it by this name. Calmet is of opinion, that kefistah was a purse of gold or silver. In the east they reckon at present by purses. The word kifla in Chaldee signifies "a measure, a vessel." And Eustathius says, that kifla is a Persian measure. Jonathan and the Targum of Jerusalem translate kefistah "a pearl." (Gen. xxxiii. 19.; Job, xlii. 11.) Or 9½ English, supposing, as Dr Prideaux does, that a shekel is worth 3 s. A daric is a piece of gold, worth, as Dr Prideaux says, 25 s. English.
