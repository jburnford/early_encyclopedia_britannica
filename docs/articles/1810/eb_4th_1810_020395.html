THESSALY

Volume 20 · 263 words · 1810 Edition

a country of Greece, whose boundaries have been different at different periods. Properly speaking, Theffily was bounded on the south by the southern parts of Greece, or Gracia Propria; east, by the Ægean; north, by Macedonia and Mygdonia; and west, by Illyricum and Epirus. It was generally divided into four separate provinces, Theffalitios, Pelagiotis, Itiatis, and Phthiotis, to which some add Magnesia. It has been severally called Æmonia, Pelagi- cum, Argos, Hellas, Argeia, Dryopis, Pelopidia, Pyrrhæa, &c. The name of Theffaly is derived from Lempris- Theffalus, one of its monarchs. Theffaly is famous for ere's Dictio- nary. A deluge which happened there in the age of Deucalion, its mountains and cities are also celebrated, such as Olympus, Pelion, Ossa, Larissa, &c. The Argonauts were partly natives of Theffaly. The inhabitants of the country palled for a treacherous nation, so that false money was called Theffalian coin, and a perfidious action a Theffalian deceit. Theffaly was originally governed by kings, till it became subject to the Macedonian monarchs. The cavalry was universally esteemed, and the people were superstitious and addicted to the study of magic and incantations. See Lucan, lib. vi. ver. 438, &c.; Dionys. 219; Curt. lib. iii. cap. 2; Aelian, Var. Hist. lib. iii. cap. 1; Pauf. lib. iv. cap. 36. lib. x. cap. 1; Mela, lib. ii. cap. 3; Justin, lib. vii. cap. 6; Diod. iv.

Theffily is now called Janina, a province of European Turkey, bounded by Macedonia on the north, by the Archipelago on the east, by Achaia or Livadia on the south, and by Epirus on the west.
