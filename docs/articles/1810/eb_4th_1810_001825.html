MARCUS HIERONYMUS, bishop of Alva, in Montferrat, and one of the most excellent Latin poets that have appeared since the Augustan age, was born at Cremona in 1470. Having distinguished himself by his learning and taste for literature, he was made bishop of Alva in 1552. After continuing two years with Pope Clement VII. at Rome, he went to reside upon his fee; where, for 30 years, he performed all the offices of a good bishop and a good man; and though he was mild, gentle, and full of goodness, he was far from wanting spirit, that when the city of Alva was besieged by the French, he used all possible means to prevent its being given up, by strenuously exhorting the people, and, when provisions were scarce, by supplying them at his own expense. His Poetics, and poem on the silk-worm, pass for his masterpiece; his poem on the game of chess is also greatly admired. He also wrote hymns, eclogues, and a poem entitled Chriftados in six books; all which are in Latin, and have gained him a great reputation. His works in prose consist of dialogues, fynodical constitutions, letters, and other pieces. He died in 1566, soon after being made bishop of Cremona.
