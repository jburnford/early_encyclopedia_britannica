DANTZIG

Volume 7 · 278 words · 1842 Edition
← DANTZICK · Index · DANUBE →

city, the capital of the government of that name in the province of West Prussia. It was an independent city, one of the Hans Towns, in ancient times, but under the protection of the kings of Poland. After the division of that country, Dantzig became subject to Prussia, and was united to it in 1793. It is a fortified city, rendered strong by its narrow front, covered by powerful batteries on the rising grounds which extend across it, and by the broad stream of the Vistula and its extensive marshes. It has been twice taken during the late war, but both times by famine rather than by the military assailants. The city, with its suburbs, contains 45,450 inhabitants, almost all of whom are engaged in commerce. The chief trade is in corn, which is brought down the Vistula from the Polish and Prussian districts on both sides of the stream. It is conveyed in open barges, landed near the city, and exposed to the sun and air till it is perfectly dry, when it is stored in vast magazines denominated speicher, till it is required for distant markets. There is also a considerable trade carried on in timber, which is also floated down from Poland. The city has been of long existence, and many of the houses exhibit the fashion of antiquity. There are appropriate institutions for education, for the poor, and for the sick. The manufactures are numerous, but all on a small scale, and chiefly for the supply of the city and its vicinity. The haven admits only vessels of small burden, or very flat bottoms. Long. 18. 33. 40. E. Lat. 54. 21. 5. N.
